Did I Have Unrealistic Expectations? Part 1

By S. Inas | Growth of a Woman

At 18, I had my whole life planned until 30. Married by 22. Boutique by 25. Done having babies by 30. Here's how that went.

In my pursuit of marriage, I knew I did not want to be like everyone else. I wanted to be realistic about life and all its possibilities. Yes, I was one of those. I set my goals according to what I saw from my family and my religious beliefs. I wanted to work in the fashion industry and then open my own little boutique by the age of 25. I wanted to get married by the age of 22 and be done having children by the age of 30. For the most part, I had a logical reason for most of my goals. For example, I got an associate's degree in fashion design, and I wanted to learn the industry and gain experience before I opened my boutique. As a religious person, in order for me to have a relationship with a man and have children, I had to get married. I was raised in the era of the independent woman, and I had no interest in that narrative, so I decided to look at life holistically.

Where Do I Begin?

Racial background: Black American
Religion: Islam
Height: 5'11"
Gender: Female
Weight: None of your business 🙂

I believe that, for the most part, everything in life is an art and has its own language. This applies to the art of relationships. Relationships are beautiful and worthy of study. In my teenage years and early twenties, I began to study Islam. I first started my journey of studying marriage and relationships by learning what my religion says about the rights of men and women in marriage.

I was blessed to be a second-generation Muslim, and I was born and raised in Brooklyn, New York. One of the perks of living in New York was access to information. Downtown Brooklyn had an Islamic bookstore on Atlantic Avenue. I could go there and browse through books, and I was able to find quite a few books on Muslim women and marriage:

  1. The Muslim Woman's Handbook by Huda Khattab
  2. Closer Than a Garment: Marital Intimacy According to the Pure Sunnah by Muhammad Mustafa al-Jibaly
  3. My Sincere Advice to Women by Umm Abdillah al-Waadi'iyyah

Even though I got asked where I was from ten times over living in NYC, the truth is my culture is American. I can't even say Black American culture, because honestly, my siblings and I were raised in a bubble. We did not have cousins and other family members around growing up, so there wasn't this Black cultural influence. That made studying Islamic guidelines on how to pursue marriage more interesting. Islam has guidelines on rights and obligations in marriage, and from reading these books, I started to formulate my own idea of what I wanted out of a relationship.

From doing my research and talking to family members, I formulated the infamous "list."

What I Wanted: The Infamous "List"

  1. A practicing Muslim man (good character)
  2. Intelligent and college educated
  3. Has a strong work ethic and has maintained a job for a few years
  4. Good communicator and conversationalist
  5. 5'11" or taller
  6. Has entrepreneurial aspirations that he is working toward
  7. Single with no children

Me at the Time

  1. A practicing Muslima
  2. Associate's degree in fashion design
  3. Freelance designer and part-time entrepreneur
  4. 5'11"
  5. A little bit of a jack of all trades and a master of none
  6. I think I'm pretty 😉
  7. No children

I've come to realize that what I might automatically assume should be part of a person's desired list for a mate may not be so. I remember talking to a colleague at my job about what she was looking for in a mate, and she said she wanted a man who did not put his hands on her. I would automatically assume that when looking for a spouse, you would be looking for someone who has integrity, honesty, good character, and high moral standards. Also, doesn't everyone want to be with someone they are attracted to? I only put height on my list because I'm 5'11", and I don't think two people who have height complexes should be together. Simply put, I have no interest in balancing out the genetic equation. Being a tall girl comes with its own set of issues, but that's another story.

In Part 2, I'll share why I wish I'd had a more present wali, the man who challenged my list, and what the search taught me about myself.
