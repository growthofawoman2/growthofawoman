One beautiful thing about Islam is that you can literally talk about everything, there is no such thing as a taboo topic in Islam. You can openly discuss anything from hygiene to sex. It is important for women to ask questions to learn her religion and learn her rights. As a Muslim woman, it is important to learn and be empowered by the fact that having a woman's consent in Islam is valued and necessary. Our parents are not allowed to marry us off without our consent.

However, we are not allowed to casually date in Islam. Islam does not endorse the concept of boyfriends and girlfriends. Islam has guidelines for how we as Muslims are to engage with the opposite sex. We are not supposed to casually carry conversations with the opposite sex. The conversation has to be straightforward pertaining to business or with the intent of marriage. Muslims around the world have their cultural way of following the Islamic rules but essentially Muslims have the equivalent of a courtship (i.e. engagement) or what we would call being intended when they are looking to get married. This whole process is supposed to be facilitated through one's guardian or protector also known as a Wali.

A woman's Wali's primary purpose is to look out for her interests and make sure that her rights are fulfilled. He is also the buffer between her and the suitor which is a protective measure. Some women may have a problem with the idea of a male guardian. But I wish I had a greater presence of a Wali because I think it would have made looking for a spouse much easier and saved me a lot of heartache. As anyone who is looking for a relationship knows, courting or dating can be an emotional rollercoaster. When you don't have a Wali as engaged as you would like you are left to decipher and deal with everything on your own. For example, if you decide that you do not want to continue talking to the suitor or negotiate a Mahr (gift from groom to bride).

## Self-Discovery

The first thing I learned in the search for a mate was that it's a process and there are three components you have to decipher. There is your heart, your mind and your gut instincts. I think your gut is the wisest one. Every interaction you have with a man is a lesson to be learned and if you look at it that way it makes the process a little easier. Okay, not really but at least you can rationalize yourself out of your emotions. Coming from a Western cultural standpoint we are looking for the "one" when the truth is the dating or engagement process is a numbers game for the lack of a better expression. The blessing is as a practicing Muslim, I am not engaging in intimate or sexual relationships with these men so even if there was a certain level of emotion there, it pales in comparison to if I did.

Nothing helps you learn what you like and do not like or what you think you want or don't want, like experience. I once spoke to a man who had children, was quite a few inches shorter than me and not financially in a position I would have preferred. I'm of the school of thought that likes to challenge what you think you really want. He was a good person and from what I could tell a practicing Muslim. He had a lot of great qualities, however when I met his children I had to truly evaluate whether that was something I wanted to be bothered with and I didn't. I come from a big family. You could say I was the main babysitter and I wanted to have my own fresh start. His height plagued many of my height insecurities. I was also looking for someone who was more financially secure because even though I had career ambitions, I wanted my focus and priority to be that of a wife and a mother. Women who believe they can do it all is a fallacy because something will suffer. We were not designed to do it all and I believe women are suffering from this ideal. That is not to say you can't have a family and a career, you just can't have it all at the same time in my opinion and do well in both areas.

Throughout my journey of getting married, I learned a great deal about myself. I learned I was impatient and God taught me patience. I got married at the age of 26 and I had my last child at the age of 36. For the most part, I learned that only I can control myself and my own expectations.

## Conclusion

Who you marry is one of the most important decisions you will make in life. I wanted to make my decision for marriage based on depth of character and faith and not on shallow physical attributes. I didn't want to marry someone just because we liked the same colors and because I thought he was cute. I have always looked at people and their relationships and wondered what they talked about before they got married. I always wondered if they talked about all the things that are considered politically incorrect like politics, religion, family structure and the truth of their financial situation. I used Islam as my foundation for my expectations. I also made my connection to God the cornerstone of my guidance. I also learned that you must test yourself to find out what you really want and manage your expectations.
